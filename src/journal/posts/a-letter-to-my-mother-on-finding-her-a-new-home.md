Dear Mom,
I have news, and I think you already know it, the way you always knew things before I said them.
I'm moving. Not far in kilometers, but far in every way that matters. And here is the part I need you to hear: you're coming with me.
The family I'm marrying into has offered you a place in their home. A space of your own for your altar, where your photograph can sit, where incense can burn on the mornings I miss you most, where fruit can be laid out on ancestral days in April the way you taught me. They offered this before I asked. They said it simply, the way people say things they mean: your parents are welcome here.
I did not know acceptance could reach backward in time. I thought it was only for the living. But they made room for my grief and my gratitude in the same gesture, and in doing so they made room for you.
You worried about me. I know you did. You asked others to look after me, because that is what mothers do when they can no longer stay. I want you to know it worked out, just not through the door any of us expected. Care found me anyway. It came through a woman who matches my heart, through her parents who treat me like a son, through a small dog who guards my feet while I have dinner. It came quietly, without conditions, without a transaction. The way you loved.
I'm building something now. A life, a home, work that belongs to me. It's small still, the way seedlings are small. But it's planted in good soil at last, and you always said that's the only thing that decides whether a thing grows.
So this letter is just to say: the incense will keep burning. The tradition continues. Your seat at the table travels with me, and the table it's traveling to is a warm one.
Come see the new house, Mom. In dreams, in April, in the smoke that curls up from the altar. You will recognize it immediately.
It looks like everything you wanted for me.
Your son,
always
